# Simplified Technical English

## Purpose

Write technical content that is accurate, direct, consistent, and difficult to misunderstand. This compact guide is based on ASD-STE100 Issue 9. It does not replace the official standard or controlled dictionary.

## Priorities

1. Preserve technical meaning and safety.
2. Remove ambiguity.
3. Use consistent terminology.
4. Reduce length only when clarity remains intact.

## Reference routing

- Read [rule-navigation.md](references/rule-navigation.md) when an exact rule location, more context, or strict compliance matters.
- Read [examples.md](references/examples.md) when you need before-and-after patterns for a revision or explanation.
- Treat both references as aids. For strict compliance, check the official PDF, its controlled dictionary, and the applicable terminology database.

## Vocabulary

- Use approved STE words when the dictionary is available.
- Use each approved word only with its approved meaning, part of speech, and form.
- Use necessary technical nouns and verbs accepted by the relevant organization or field.
- Use one term for one item. Do not alternate between synonyms.
- Avoid slang, jargon, regional wording, idioms, and phrasal verbs.
- Use American English spelling unless an official requirement specifies otherwise.
- Rewrite the sentence when a word-for-word substitution is not clear.

## Nouns

- Keep multi-word nouns to three words or fewer.
- When an official term is longer, write it in full first. Then define a clear short form or use hyphens to mark words that function as one unit.
- Prefer prepositions or relative clauses to long noun stacks.
- Use `a`, `an`, `the`, `this`, or `these` where needed.

## Verbs

- Use active voice and name the actor.
- Use a verb to express an action, not an abstract noun phrase.
- Use only the infinitive, imperative, simple present, simple past, simple future, and permitted past-participle forms.
- Avoid complex auxiliary constructions.
- In descriptive text, use passive voice only when the actor is unknown.
- Use an `-ing` form only as an accepted technical noun or modifier in a technical noun.

## Sentences and lists

- Write complete, direct sentences. Do not use contractions or omit necessary words.
- Put one main idea in each sentence.
- Use a vertical list for several conditions, items, or actions.
- Use explicit connecting words when the relation between sentences is not clear.
- Do not use semicolons.

## Procedures

- Use no more than 20 words in a sentence.
- Put one instruction in each sentence, except when actions occur simultaneously.
- Begin an instruction with an imperative verb: `Remove`, `Install`, `Measure`.
- Put a necessary condition before the command and separate it with a comma.
- Use notes for information only. Do not put instructions in notes.

## Descriptions

- Use no more than 25 words in a sentence.
- Give information gradually, from general context to specific detail.
- Give each paragraph one topic and no more than six sentences.
- Repeat key terms when repetition prevents ambiguity.

## Safety instructions

- Identify the risk level with the approved signal word or symbol.
- In ASD usage, a **warning** identifies risk of injury or death. A **caution** identifies risk of damage to objects.
- Start with a clear command or condition.
- State the hazard or possible result explicitly.

## Final check

Confirm that:

- each instruction has one clear action.
- each pronoun has one clear referent.
- each item has one consistent name.
- sentence and paragraph limits are met.
- safety consequences are explicit.
- units, identifiers, labels, quotations, and technical facts are unchanged.
- no simplification creates ambiguity.

For strict compliance, check every general word against the official dictionary and every technical term against the applicable terminology database.
