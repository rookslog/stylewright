## One instruction per sentence

**Before**

> Remove the cover and inspect the seal and replace it if it is damaged.

**After**

> Remove the cover.
>
> Inspect the seal.
>
> If the seal is damaged, replace the seal.

**Why**

Each sentence gives one instruction. The condition applies only to replacement, and repetition removes pronoun ambiguity.

## Condition before command

**Before**

> Close the supply valve when the pressure is less than 20 kPa.

**After**

> When the pressure is less than 20 kPa, close the supply valve.

**Why**

The reader receives the necessary condition before the command. The value and unit do not change.

## Active voice and a named actor

**Before**

> The inspection report must be signed before the aircraft is released.

**After**

> Before the maintenance manager releases the aircraft, the maintenance manager must sign the inspection report.

**Why**

The revision names the actor for each action. Use the approved role name from the applicable procedure.

## Action verbs instead of abstract nouns

**Before**

> Perform an examination of the cable for signs of damage.

**After**

> Examine the cable for damage.

**Why**

The verb states the action directly. The revision removes an unnecessary noun phrase without changing the object of the examination.

## Shorter multi-word nouns

**Before**

> Examine the hydraulic pump outlet pressure indication line.

**After**

> Examine the indication line for the outlet pressure of the hydraulic pump.

**Why**

Prepositional phrases separate the relationships in the noun stack. Confirm that the revised relationships match the product data.

When a long official term cannot change, introduce it before a short form:

> The hydraulic-pump outlet-pressure indication line (the indication line) is below the pump. Examine the indication line.

## One term for one item

**Before**

> Remove the access cover. Put the panel on a clean surface. Examine the plate for cracks.

**After**

> Remove the access cover. Put the access cover on a clean surface. Examine the access cover for cracks.

**Why**

One item has one name. Repetition is preferable to synonyms when the synonyms can suggest different parts.

## Unambiguous pronouns

**Before**

> Disconnect the hose from the pump and examine it.

**After — examine the hose**

> Disconnect the hose from the pump. Examine the hose.

**After — examine the pump**

> Disconnect the hose from the pump. Examine the pump.

**Why**

The source sentence does not identify the object of `it`. Select the applicable revision only after the technical intent is known.

## Procedure and description limits

### Procedure

**Before**

> After you make sure that the electrical power is off, carefully remove the two bolts that attach the pump to the housing.

**After**

> Make sure that the electrical power is off.
>
> Remove the two bolts that attach the pump to the housing.

**Why**

The revision uses short imperative sentences and keeps one instruction in each sentence. Count a procedural sentence against the 20-word limit.

### Description

**Before**

> The controller, which receives temperature data from three sensors and compares the data with the selected limit, sends a signal to the shutdown relay when the temperature is too high.

**After**

> The controller receives temperature data from three sensors. It compares the data with the selected limit. If the temperature is too high, the controller sends a signal to the shutdown relay.

**Why**

The revision gives information gradually and separates related ideas. Count each descriptive sentence against the 25-word limit.

## Warning and caution

### Injury or death risk

**Before**

> CAUTION: Disconnect the power because the voltage can cause an injury.

**After**

> WARNING: Disconnect the electrical power before you touch the terminals. High voltage can cause injury or death.

**Why**

In ASD usage, an injury or death risk requires a warning, not a caution. Confirm the approved signal word, command, hazard, and consequence with the applicable safety source.

### Object-damage risk

**Before**

> WARNING: Do not use a metal tool because it can scratch the lens.

**After**

> CAUTION: Do not use a metal tool on the lens. A metal tool can damage the lens.

**Why**

The stated consequence is damage to an object. Confirm that no injury risk is omitted before selecting a caution.

## Vertical lists and connecting words

### Vertical list

**Before**

> Make sure that the kit contains a gasket, two clamps, a hose, and four bolts and that none of these parts is damaged.

**After**

> Make sure that the kit contains these parts:
>
> - one gasket
> - two clamps
> - one hose
> - four bolts.
>
> Make sure that the parts are not damaged.

**Why**

The list makes the quantities easy to check. Apply the official punctuation and word-count rules before release.

### Connecting words

**Before**

> The indicator is red. The pressure is too high.

**After**

> The indicator is red because the pressure is too high.

**Why**

The connecting word states the relationship. Use it only when the cause-and-effect relation is verified.

## Compliance boundary

These examples are patterns for revision and explanation. For strict compliance, check the applicable writing rules, every general word in the official dictionary, and every technical term in the applicable terminology database.
