# The commitments behind the measurement design

A companion to the measurement design of 2026-08-04.

The mechanisms are the framework. This page indexes them, so a design
argument can ask whether a change keeps a commitment instead of
relitigating each mechanism alone. Each commitment names its source,
because the literature already records the failure modes we would
otherwise rediscover.

## A claim earns credit only from a test it could have failed

Mayo calls this severity, and it is the standard here, not naive
falsification. A run that cannot come out against the skill proves nothing
when it comes out for it. This is why the control arms exist, why the
probe has an empty-home control, and why the scorer refuses mismatched
provenance. One honesty note bounds the whole commitment: at the
repetition floor the runner enforces, severity is asserted, not estimated.
No sensitivity analysis exists yet, and the design says so rather than
implying one.

## A skill answers an observed failure

The workflow writes a skill only against a failure a control shows, and
stops when the control is clean — with one documented exception for a
failure that is real only in a fuller stack, which gets a declared
representative-stack control instead. That is Popper's instinct. Two
limits from his successors apply, and the design concedes both.

Duhem and Quine: no test confronts the skill alone. It confronts the skill
together with the harness, the serving configuration, the isolation, and
the selector. An installed treatment that resembles its control does not
say which link failed, and no single record localises blame — the probe,
after the fact, localises discoverability alone, and selection, loading,
and effect stay entangled.

Lakatos: a failed treatment does not kill the idea behind the skill, and a
rewrite deserves another run. The attempt ledger is what separates a
progressing programme from a degenerating one. A skill rewritten five
times with no measured gain looks like exactly that.

## The record precedes the result

Selective reporting is the default failure of self-run experiments. So the
ledger entry that precedes any treatment arm names the rubric, the
scenario frame and its selection, the primary metric, the primary
scenario, the repetition count, the predicted direction, and the stopping
rule — and every arm attempt enters the ledger before it starts,
exploratory included. The ledger binds only what enters it: an
unregistered attempt leaves no trace any check can find, so the ledger
makes selective reporting a deliberate breach of a stated rule, never an
impossibility, and the design says so. Every arm is promoted including the failures, and a
promoted study is tamper-evident under its digests. Ordering is not
self-attested: the public push time is the clock on both ends — a
registration counts only when pushed before the evidence it governs was
first pushed — verified while the server still attests it. Execution time
stays the author's own fact, and the design says so instead of claiming
more.

## Results survive their own multiplicity

A study prints many numbers, and an author who may pick after looking will
pick the one that moved. Forking paths and optional stopping are the two
doors, and the pre-registered primary endpoint and stopping rule are the
two locks. Everything not pre-registered is reported as secondary and
labelled as such.

## A metric is not the construct

The scorer counts shapes, and the only shape that has ever separated a
pair in this bench is word count. A measured claim built on that alone
reduces to "shorter", which is not the construct any skill teaches. The
retained human judgment is what carries the construct, and the derived
status line carries the study's conclusion rather than a bare "measured",
so existence of a study never impersonates a result.

## Judgment stays human, and it is retained whole

Kuhn: observation is theory-laden. The rubric embodies a view of what
counts as failure, and no procedure purifies it. Pre-registration
timestamps the view. What retention buys is not an unbiased gate — the
judge is still the author — but argument: a stranger gets the samples, the
rubric, the labels, and the exclusions, and can re-judge every claim
later. Auditability, not authority, is the defence.

## A measure that becomes a target stops measuring

Goodhart and Campbell both recorded this. The scorer alone is maximisable,
so the number is never sufficient for a claim: the pre-registered rubric,
the retained judgment, and the committed samples are what a gamed metric
cannot survive, because anyone can re-read them.

## A claim binds to its conditions

No result generalises past its harness, build, served model, platform,
pathway, and environment class. The probe records its scope and its
cadence is calendar-driven. Status lines are derived, digest-bound, and
computed stale when the skill or the harness moves. An old figure is
superseded per claim by a person, and retired from tables into prose when
the first retained study lands — the record kept, the credential expired.

## Observation and experiment are different instruments

Field signal shows which failures occur in real use. It has no control
arm, so it confirms nothing. It enters at the exploratory step, never the
confirmatory one. The bench tests.
